# Clear transcription controls

Version 0.33 groups Recording language and Transcript output in one rounded settings block. Each row separates its label from the selected value and shows a trailing chevron. Quality choices share one compact list with radio indicators, subtle selected tint, and a technical model/download/language caption. Unselected indicators use a quiet neutral color. Detailed explanations remain in About these options; selecting a model still does not silently download or replace saved text.

The transcript's quality selector and Update transcript action have compact soft filled shapes inside their existing 48 dp touch targets. Their backgrounds distinguish them from headings and explanatory text without accent outlines. The first-time setup/download action remains primary. Secondary buttons share one tinted treatment; model removal, About, and greeting actions no longer remove their button backgrounds. Toolbar icons, playback speed, and contextual Skip controls retain their established locations and feedback.

Language/output and model changes are disabled and visibly dimmed during active speech work. Readable scaling, Light/Dark/AMOLED colors, native button/radio accessibility semantics, immediate actions, press feedback, and Reduce motion remain supported.

Below the media player, one rounded transcript surface contains its heading, quality selector, search, output tabs, status/update action, and continuous text. It has no outline or separate phrase cards. Search and output controls use recessed backgrounds; only the playing phrase receives a subtle highlight. Changed-quality status and Update share a row when space allows, then stack at larger font sizes or narrow widths. Saved text and tap-to-play timing are preserved.

This uses [Apple's button guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons) for visible standalone controls and consistent hierarchy, and [Samsung's One UI guide](https://design.samsung.com/global/contents/one-ui/download/oneui_design_guide_eng.pdf), pages 35 and 41â€“42, for contained body actions and grouped title/secondary-text lists. These are applied principles, not a claim of visual equivalence or platform endorsement.

The emulator-only TranscriptionControlsCheck refuses carrier sessions/non-sample inbox entries, checks the grouped values/chevrons and native selection, and captures both pages in all three themes. Captures use sample data and are reviewed before documentation use. Preferences are restored after capture. Existing quality/motion, first-use, and small-window/large-text checks supplement this visual review.

Validation on the S24-sized API 36 emulator: 53 control/theme assertions and 514 adaptive-layout assertions across 138 visible controls passed. The 35 retained-selection checks and 102 per-frame layout assertions also passed. Host behavior and 24 theme contrast combinations passed. A software-rendered run of the quality/motion check passed its transcription and geometry checks but failed the strict 180 ms tap-delivery limit; rapid phrase playback also failed its fixed-delay check under that load. The S24 Ultra then passed all 39 quality/motion, 47 recording/playback, 19 physical transition, and 35 selection checks (140 total), including immediate tap delivery, rapid phrase seeks, search, and observed checkbox animation. The physical long-press run sampled 31 rendered frames, with a largest list movement of 74 pixels across a 927-pixel header. Device results establish those paths; the emulator timing failures remain recorded rather than counted as passes.
